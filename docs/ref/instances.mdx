## What each family is for

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="t3 — burstable" icon="battery-half">
    The **straggler generator**. Cheap, and only sustains a fraction of a vCPU once
    its credits are gone. Put one in an otherwise uniform fleet and find out whether
    your placement notices.
  </Card>
  <Card title="m5 — balanced" icon="scale-balanced">
    The default. Use it when the point of the scenario is not the machines.
  </Card>
  <Card title="c5 — compute" icon="gauge-high">
    More cores per franc, less memory. `c5.4xlarge` is the one to reach for when you
    want real contention inside one machine — sixteen threads on one pool.
  </Card>
  <Card title="r5 — memory" icon="memory">
    Twice the memory of an `m5` at the same core count. The shape for a design that
    holds things.
  </Card>
</Columns>

`i3.large` is the storage-shaped one — 475 GB against everyone else's 8 to 32.

## What losim derives from the row

<ParamField path="vCPU" type="int">
  The size of the machine's **thread pool**, and its speed relative to the reference
  machine: `machineFactor = 2 ÷ vcpu`. A `c5.4xlarge` runs a `@Takes(refMs = 8)`
  handler in 1 refMs; a `t3.small` takes the full 8.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="memory" type="MB">
  The default memory cap, enforced against the retained-heap walk. Overridable per
  machine with `memoryMb:` in direct mode; **solved by the engine** in scaled mode.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="storage" type="GB">
  The default disk cap. `wroteDisk` throws past it.
</ParamField>

<ParamField path="$/hour" type="double">
  The on-demand rate, used by the bill's **capacity** bucket. A machine that received
  a `spot_notice` is billed at the spot rate — 70% off by default.
</ParamField>

<Note>
**What a machine costs to rent lives here, not in the price list.** It is a property
of the machine, beside its vCPUs and its memory, rather than a choice the course
makes. Two places declaring what an `r5.large` costs would be two places to drift
apart, and the drift would be silent.
</Note>

## Burstable machines

`t3` instances carry `baselineFraction` and `creditSeconds`: a sustained share of a
vCPU, and how many seconds of full speed they get first. The throttled factor is
`cpuFactor ÷ baselineFraction` — so a `t3.small` at 20% baseline is **five times**
slower than its core count suggests once its credits are gone.

## Choosing them

<Tip>
The most instructive fleet is not a uniform one:

```yaml
workers:
  count: 4
  prefix: w
  instance: c5.large
  runs: [lab.Shrinker]
  overrides:
    w3: { instance: t3.small }
```

Three compute machines and one that will fall behind. Whether your coordinator
notices — and what it does about it — is the whole exercise, and a fleet of four
identical machines cannot ask the question.
</Tip>
