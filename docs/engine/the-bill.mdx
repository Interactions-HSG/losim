Generic over labs: it reads only the trace, so any gRPC system gets a cost view
without anyone writing one for it.

## Four buckets, printed apart

| | what it is a model of |
|---|---|
| `capacity` | the system, for the period. An idle node costs as much as a busy one. |
| `consumption` | what the work burned: cross-zone egress, storage |
| `incidents` | zero until something breaks, then large |
| `build` | engineering time to construct and carry this design, per distinct service |

<Note>
Adding them together would hide the trade every design makes. Replication triples
capacity and adds to build in order to empty incidents — and one number cannot say
that.

A line appears only when there is a quantity to put in it. A bucket nobody could fill
is not a bucket that cost nothing.
</Note>

## Capacity is priced in reference time

The run took a few seconds of your afternoon. The job it models takes what the
simulated clock says, and **that** is the number anyone would be invoiced for.

A node that received a `spot_notice` is billed at the spot rate — 70% off by
default, so choosing reclaimable capacity is a trade rather than a hazard.

## Two bills for a scaled run

Once for what happened, and once for the job it models.

<Warning>
A bill is **quantities times prices**, and the engine will not project every quantity.

Capacity depends on the timeline. The timeline is the noisiest thing losim measures
so the capacity line is routinely refused.
</Warning>

```text
  capacity    the system, for the period            -           CHF   refused
      its exponent moves by 0.793 between independent seed sets of the same
      workload, which over a factor of 6 is an error bar of x4.1 — wider than
      anything it would be asked to distinguish
```

So the honest account says: the bytes cost this much, the storage costs this much,
and nobody can tell you what the nodes cost, because nobody can tell you how long
the job takes.

<Note>
That is useful because it shows where the uncertainty sits.
</Note>

## Each quantity carries its own factor

Resources do not share one factor. Cross-zone bytes are grown by the wire law rather
than a law of their own. What crosses a zone is a property of the topology, not of
the size, so the share stays fixed and the total moves. If the wire law itself was
refused, the line is refused rather than printed with an observed quantity under a
full-scale heading.

## Incidents are never projected

A node either failed or it did not. An incident bucket extrapolated from one
afternoon would be a forecast dressed as an observation.

## Prices are course data

They live in `prices/`, outside the simulator, so they can be updated or distorted
for an exercise. Billing is a pure function of the trace, so re-billing under a
different price list costs nothing and needs no re-run.

<Warning>
What a node costs to rent is not in the price list. That belongs to the instance
catalogue, beside its vCPUs and its memory, because it is a property of the node.
Two places declaring what an `r5.large` costs would drift apart, and the drift would
be silent.
</Warning>
