Generic over labs: it reads only the trace, so any gRPC system gets a cost view
without anyone writing one for it.

## Four buckets, printed apart

| | what it is a model of |
|---|---|
| `capacity` | the fleet, for the period — an idle machine costs exactly as much as a busy one |
| `consumption` | what the work burned: cross-zone egress, storage |
| `incidents` | zero until something breaks, then large |
| `build` | engineering time to construct and carry this design, per distinct service |

<Note>
Adding them together would hide the trade every design makes. Replication triples
capacity and adds to build in order to empty incidents — and one number cannot say
that.

A line appears only when there is a quantity to put in it. A bucket nobody could fill
in is not a bucket that cost nothing, and the total says explicitly that it is a total
**of what could be priced**.
</Note>

## Capacity is priced in reference time

The run took a few seconds of your afternoon. The job it is a model of takes what the
simulated clock says, and **that** is the number anyone would be invoiced for.

A machine that received a `spot_notice` is billed at the spot rate — 70% off by
default — so choosing reclaimable capacity is a genuine trade rather than a hazard.

## Two bills for a scaled run

Once for what happened, and once for the job it is a model of.

<Warning>
A bill is **quantities times prices**, and the engine will not project every quantity.

Capacity depends on the timeline. The timeline is the noisiest thing losim measures
and is routinely refused. And capacity is usually the **largest line on the bill**.
</Warning>

```
  capacity    the fleet, for the period            -           CHF   refused
      its exponent moves by 0.793 between independent seed sets of the same
      workload, which over a factor of 6 is an error bar of x4.1 — wider than
      anything it would be asked to distinguish
```

So the honest account routinely says: *the bytes cost this much, the storage costs
this much, and nobody can tell you what the machines cost, because nobody can tell you
how long the job takes.*

<Note>
**That is a useful answer.** It says where the uncertainty in the cost of a design
sits.
</Note>

## Each quantity carries its own factor

Because that is the whole point of the engine: resources do not share one.

Cross-zone bytes are grown by the **wire** law rather than a law of their own — which
calls cross a zone is a property of the topology and not of the size, so the share is
held fixed and the total is what moves. If the wire law itself was refused, the line is
refused rather than printed with an observed quantity under a full-scale heading.

## Incidents are never projected

A machine either failed or it did not. An incident bucket extrapolated from one
afternoon would be a forecast dressed as an observation.

## Prices are course data

They live in `prices/`, outside the simulator, so they can be updated or deliberately
distorted for an exercise. Billing is a **pure function of the trace**, so re-billing
under a different price list costs nothing and needs no re-run — which makes "is this
design's advantage real, or is it a price assumption?" a one-command question.

<Warning>
**What a machine costs to rent is not in the price list.** That belongs to the
instance catalogue, beside its vCPUs and its memory, because it is a property of the
machine rather than a choice the course makes. Two places declaring what an
`r5.large` costs would be two places to drift apart, and the drift would be silent.
</Warning>
