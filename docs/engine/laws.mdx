```
resource = fixed + coefficient × variable ^ beta
```

```
memoryMb = 0.0597 + 0.00187 * revealed.distinctItems^1.013  (R2 1.0000, wobble 0.000)
```

| term | |
|---|---|
| `fixed` | overhead that is real at every scale. A JVM's baseline heap does not shrink because the workload did. |
| `coefficient`, `beta` | the variable part |
| `variable` | what the resource turned out to be a function of |
| `R2` | how well the line went through the points it was given |
| `wobble` | how far the exponent moves when the whole thing is fitted again on independent seeds |

## Candidate variables

Every quantity that **varied** across the ladder and could plausibly drive something:

- `records` — what the scenario declared
- every numeric `Losim.current().reveal(key, value)` your code made

Each resource is fitted against **every** candidate, and the best is kept.

<Note>
This is why `reveal` is worth using well. `reveal("distinctItems", index.size())`
gives the engine the chance to discover that memory follows what you hold rather than
what went past — and those grow at different rates.
</Note>

## The two-step composition

A variable that is not `records` is **itself fitted against records**:

```
memoryMb      = f(distinctItems)
distinctItems = g(records)
```

So the projection composes two well-behaved laws instead of one fragile one. The
variable's own uncertainty rides along, **raised to the resource's exponent**, so the
composed law's certainty is limited by the certainty of the quantity it is built on.

## Choosing the best candidate

Not by R². In order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Would it be reproduced?">
    A usable candidate has an error bar within limits **and** a ladder that does not
    bend. Unusable candidates lose to usable ones outright.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Which has the narrower error bar?">
    Compared to within 0.01.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A tie goes to `records`">
    Because `records` is known **exactly** at full scale rather than itself being
    projected.

    Anything else has to earn its place by **halving** the variance the simpler
    answer leaves unexplained. Otherwise a variable that is merely records-plus-a-
    constant wins on a hair of R² and then extrapolates its own offset as though it
    were growth.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Only then, R²">
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
That order is deliberate. **R² measures how well a line fits the points already
taken; the question here is whether it would hold for the next ones.**
</Warning>

## Wobble, and the error bar

```java
wobble  = the spread of beta across independent seed sets
errorBar = scaleFactor ^ (wobble + carried)
```

An uncertainty of `wobble` in the exponent becomes a factor of `F^wobble` in the
answer, where `F` is how far the projection reaches. Over a factor of 5000, a wobble
of 0.66 is an error bar of **×268**.

Past **×2.0** the projection no longer distinguishes anything a reader would have
wanted distinguished, and it is refused.

<Note>
Wobble is measured **per host and per workload** rather than assumed, because a
two-core Codespace is noisier than a laptop and the same constant would not serve
both.
</Note>

## A law per cost site, not one for the whole run

A hash lookup is flat in `n`; a per-record scan is linear; a sort is `n log n`; a
fan-out is quadratic in the fleet. One exponent for all of them would be wrong for
most.

Cost-site laws are kept only where they would **survive being measured again**. On
handlers shorter than the host's own jitter, that is usually nowhere — which is
exactly what the `k_time` feasibility check reads.

## Fault amplification

Each resource carries a multiplier derived from the fault column: how much more of it
the weathered runs demanded than the clean ones. Applied at projection time, and
printed beside the law when it is meaningfully above 1:

```
  memoryMb   = 0.0597 + 0.00187 * revealed.distinctItems^1.013  +-x1.10  x1.97 under fault
```

## The plan travels

Every law, every variable-of-records law, every refusal and every solved cap is
written into the trace — so `projected = f(observed)` is recomputable by whoever
reads it, rather than a number they have to take on trust.
