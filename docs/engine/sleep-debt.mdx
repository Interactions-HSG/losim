## The timer floor

The clock cannot express a duration finer than about **0.05 ms** of real time. At
the engine's maximum compression of 40x, a declared cost of 1 refMs is 25 µs and
falls below that floor. At 20x, a cost of 0.5 refMs has the same result.

Rounding those costs up slows the run. Dropping them shortens it. Both choices
distort the measured duration.

## Debt accounting

A cost finer than the floor is **owed** instead of slept:

```java
owed += refMs / kTime;
if (owed < FLOOR) return;            // nothing to sleep yet
park(owed / correction);
owed -= actuallySlept;               // settle against reality, not intent
```

The debt is kept per **thread**. It accumulates until it is large enough to express,
then is paid in one park.

## Closed-loop accounting

<Note>
The debt is settled against what was **actually** slept. The requested duration is
used only as the initial target.

With open-loop accounting, the calibration's residual compounds across thousands of
tiny sleeps, producing **6.8% aggregate error typically and 16% at worst**. Crediting
the overshoot back shortens later sleeps to absorb it, reducing the error to **0.3%
and 2%**.
</Note>

Sub-floor costs therefore contribute to total time without forcing `k_time` down
to the timer resolution.

## Preserved and unavailable

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Kept" icon="check">
    **Aggregate time.** A hundred 0.001 refMs costs still add up to 0.1 refMs.

    **Ordering.** Events happen in the order they happened.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Lost" icon="xmark">
    **Per-call observability.** The trace cannot recover where a particular cost fell
    inside the debt window because several sub-floor costs are paid off together.
  </Card>
</Columns>

The trace records this limitation. The engine checks `k_time` against the finest
fitted cost site and refuses a run when the timing would drift.

## The limit

The ledger is good for about **fifty times** below the floor. Past that the residual
stops being absorbed and the run's own timings drift.

```
this run is clocked at 40x real time, and lab.Thumbnailer.Capacity costs 0.0004
refMs, 6x below what the sleep debt can still settle at that compression. Its
timings would drift instead of being owed. Give that cost site more to do, or lower
the scale: at scale 1 the clock runs at 20x.
```

<Tip>
Choose one:

**Lower `k_time`.** Lowering `k_time` makes the run take longer in real time while
leaving the other values unchanged.

**Give that cost site more to do.** A handler declared at 0.5 refMs may be below the
noise floor of the modeled operation. Consider whether it should be annotated or
declared at its measured cost.
</Tip>

## Per-host calibration

```java
double[] targets = {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2};
```

The calibration uses two hundred repetitions at the short end and sixty at the long
end. The mean ratio of *slept* to *asked for* is the correction. A host that never
overshoots gets a correction of 1.

<Note>
The correction is measured per host because timer behavior varies between hosts.
`losim compare` therefore treats durations as **measurements**, not structural values
that must agree across environments.
</Note>
