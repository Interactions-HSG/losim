<Note>
**A projection carries its confidence, or it is absent.** A field filled with a
plausible number is indistinguishable from a good one until the cluster bill arrives.

So the engine tries to break every law before printing it, and where it succeeds the
projected column says why instead of what.
</Note>

## Test 1 — does the ladder bend?

Split the ladder in half. Fit the lower half and the upper half separately. Compare
the exponents.

```
diskMb  REFUSED: the ladder bends: over the lower half it grows as records^0.59 and
over the upper half as records^0.87, a difference of 0.28. Something behaves
differently small than large — a threshold, a different code path, or a granularity
that only shows up when the pieces are few — and no extrapolation across that means
anything. R2 over the whole ladder is still 0.883, which is why no threshold on R2
could have separated this from a merely noisy straight line.
```

<Warning>
**R² cannot make this call.** On a workload that spills above a key count, R² over
the whole ladder falls only to about **0.74** — a value a merely noisy *linear*
workload scores just as easily.

That is why the refusal quotes the R² in its own text. A reader who reaches for R²
instead — and it is the obvious thing to reach for — should be able to see from the
refusal itself why it would not have served.
</Warning>

The test is applied on the axis the ladder was climbed on, **records**. A resource
that bends against records has changed behaviour between the small end and the large
end, and no choice of variable makes that legitimate — re-parameterising a bend only
hides it, and the hidden version extrapolates confidently and wrongly.

## Test 2 — would the exponent land in the same place again?

Refit on independent seed sets: same workload, same sizes, different random draws.
The spread is the error bar.

```
makespan  REFUSED: its exponent moves by 0.66 between independent seed sets of the
same workload, which over a factor of 5000 is an error bar of x268 — wider than
anything it would be asked to distinguish
```

R² says how well a line went through the points it was given. It says nothing about
whether those points would land there again.

<Note>
Measured: on short handlers the **time** exponent moves by about 0.25 between
independent seed sets of an identical workload, while **memory** does not move at
all. The engine measures that per host and per workload rather than assuming a
constant.
</Note>

## The order matters

An exponent that cannot be reproduced cannot establish that a ladder bends either. So
an unreproducible measurement is refused **as unreproducible**, rather than as a
discontinuity it has no standing to claim.

## Refusals about the whole run

These stop the run before it happens.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="the variable part does not dominate the fixed overhead">
    ```
    memoryMb at 8000 records is only 2.4x its own fixed overhead (0.31 against 0.13);
    below 10x the fit is describing the overhead rather than the workload
    ```

    Checked only for the resources the solve actually caps — memory and disk. A large
    fixed term elsewhere (allocation carries the JVM's own warm-up, which is real at
    every scale) says the law for *that* resource is weak, not that the run size is
    wrong, and refusing a whole run over it would leave nothing runnable at all.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="the fleet would not fit this host">
    ```
    at 8000 records the fleet would hold 4200 MB, and this host offers 1843 MB to
    work in — the run does not fit the laptop it is meant to fit on
    ```

    The engine will use at most **60%** of the JVM's max heap.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="k_time is finer than the workload can express">
    The finest fitted cost site, against the sleep debt's fifty-times-below-the-floor
    limit. The message computes the largest `k_time` that works.
    [The sleep debt →](/engine/the-sleep-debt)
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

When there is no feasible size at all:

```
thumbs-scaled.yaml  scaled mode: no feasible size
  <the reason>
  Nothing was run and nothing is projected, which is the point:
  a projection that could not be made must be absent, not guessed.
```

## Refusals reach the bill

Capacity depends on the timeline. When the timeline is refused, so is the capacity
line — and it quotes the engine's own reason rather than paraphrasing it:

```
  capacity    the fleet, for the period            -           CHF   refused
      its exponent moves by 0.793 between independent seed sets of the same
      workload, which over a factor of 6 is an error bar of x4.1
```

Cross-zone egress is grown by the **wire** law rather than one of its own — which
calls cross a zone is a property of the topology, not of the size. If the wire law was
refused, that line is refused too, rather than printing an observed quantity under a
full-scale heading.

## What not to do about a refusal

<Warning>
**There is no threshold to lower.** The tests are structural — does the ladder bend,
is the exponent reproducible — not a tunable confidence level.

And do not fall back on multiplying the small run by the size ratio. That is
precisely what the engine refused to do, and on a workload where memory follows
something sublinear it lands 34% out.
</Warning>
