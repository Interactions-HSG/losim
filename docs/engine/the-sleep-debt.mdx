## The problem

The clock cannot express a duration finer than about **0.05 ms** of real time. At
`kTime: 40`, a declared cost of 1 refMs is 25 µs — comfortably below it. At
`kTime: 100`, so is a cost of 4 refMs.

Rounding those up would make the run slower than it should be. Dropping them would
make it faster. Both distort exactly the thing the run is measuring.

## The mechanism

A cost finer than the floor is **owed** rather than slept:

```java
owed += refMs / kTime;
if (owed < FLOOR) return;            // nothing to sleep yet
park(owed / correction);
owed -= actuallySlept;               // settle against reality, not intent
```

The debt is per **thread** — a debt is a property of whoever incurred it — and it
accumulates until it is large enough to express, then is paid off in one park.

## Why the ledger is closed-loop

<Note>
The debt is settled against what was **actually** slept, not against what was asked
for.

Open-loop, the calibration's residual compounds across thousands of tiny sleeps:
**6.8% aggregate error typically and 16% at worst**. Crediting the overshoot back, so
the next sleeps shorten to absorb it, brings that to **0.3% and 2%**.
</Note>

That is what makes costs fifty times below the floor still total correctly, and it is
why `k_time` is not capped by timer resolution.

## What is lost, and what is not

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Kept" icon="check">
    **Aggregate time.** A hundred 0.001 refMs costs still add up to 0.1 refMs.

    **Ordering.** Events happen in the order they happened.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Lost" icon="xmark">
    **Per-call observability.** Where inside the debt window a particular cost fell
    is not recoverable — several sub-floor costs are paid off together.
  </Card>
</Columns>

The trace has to say so, which is why the engine checks the `k_time` against the
finest cost site it fitted rather than letting it drift silently.

## The limit, and the message

The ledger is good for about **fifty times** below the floor. Past that the residual
stops being absorbed and the run's own timings drift.

```
k_time 400 is finer than this workload can express: lab.Thumbnailer.Capacity costs
0.5000 refMs, which at that compression is 4x below what the sleep debt can still
settle. Lower k_time to 100 or less, or give that cost site more to do.
```

<Tip>
Two ways out, and they are genuinely different decisions:

**Lower `k_time`.** The run takes longer in real time, and everything else is
unchanged.

**Give that cost site more to do.** If a handler is declared at 0.5 refMs, it is
below the noise floor of the thing it is modelling anyway — and a `@Takes` that small
usually means the handler should not have been annotated at all, or should be
declared at what it really costs.
</Tip>

## Calibration is per host

```java
double[] targets = {0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2};
```

Two hundred repetitions at the short end, sixty at the long end, and the mean ratio
of *slept* to *asked for* is the correction. A host that never overshoots gets a
correction of 1.

<Note>
Measured per host rather than assumed, because a two-core Codespace behaves
differently from a laptop and the same constant would not serve both. It is also why
`losim diff` treats every duration as a **measurement** rather than something that
must agree across environments.
</Note>
